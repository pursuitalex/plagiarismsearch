This page is a reference, not a legal document: it holds every element the legal document body styles, written as plain HTML with no class on anything. It exists so the legal typography can be checked in one place, on a phone and on a desktop, before a document is moved into the template.

A second paragraph follows the first at the document's rhythm. It carries a link inside the text, a term in bold, a phrase in italics, a footnote sign1 and a name in inline_code, so each can be seen against ordinary text.

1. A second-level heading opens a section of the document

The paragraph under a heading sits close to it, and the heading sits well clear of what came before. Each section heading carries an id, so the contents of the page can link to it and the heading stops below the fixed header.

1.1. A third-level heading divides a section

It is smaller and lighter than the heading above it, and larger and heavier than the text under it.

A fourth-level heading names a clause inside a subsection

It is set close to the size of body text, in bold.

A fifth-level heading is rarely needed

When a document uses one, it stays a heading and stays quieter than the fourth level.

A sixth-level heading reads as a label

It is set small, in capitals.

2. A very long second-level heading, of the kind a legal document is full of, that runs past the end of one line on a desktop and across four or five on a phone

2.1. A third-level heading placed directly under a second-level heading

Each heading keeps its own space above, as it does on the approved pages.

6. A quotation, preformatted text and a rule

A quoted provision is set off by a rule at its side:

"Personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.

Preformatted text scrolls sideways rather than widening the page:

Set-Cookie: session_id=EXAMPLE; Path=/; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Max-Age=0; Domain=.example.com

A horizontal rule separates two parts of a document without a heading. Small print is set a little smaller than the text it sits in.
