This page is a reference, not a guide: it holds every element the User Guide article body styles, written as plain HTML with no class on anything. It exists so the guide typography can be checked in one place, on a phone and on a desktop, before an article from the archive is moved into the template.

A second paragraph follows the first at the body rhythm. It carries a link inside the text, a phrase in bold, a phrase in italics, a marked phrase, a footnote sign1 and a line of inline code, so each can be seen against ordinary text.

A second-level heading opens a section of the article

The paragraph under a heading sits close to it, and the heading sits well clear of what came before. Each section heading carries an id, so "On this page" can link to it and the heading stops below the fixed header.

A third-level heading divides a section

It is smaller and lighter than the heading above it, and larger and heavier than the text under it.

A fourth-level heading names a point inside a subsection

It is set at the size of body text, in bold.

A fifth-level heading is rarely needed

When an old article uses one, it stays a heading and stays quieter than the fourth level.

A sixth-level heading reads as a label

It is set small, in capitals.

A very long second-level heading, of the kind an archive is full of, that runs past the end of one line on a desktop and across four or five on a phone

A third-level heading placed directly under a second-level heading

Each heading keeps its own space above, as it does in the approved article.

Lists

An unordered list takes the article's tick; an ordered list takes its numbered disc.

  • A short item.
  • An item that opens in bold. The rest of the sentence runs on in the same line of text, and wraps under itself rather than under the marker when it is long enough to need a second line.
  • An item with a link in it.
  • An item that holds a list of its own:
    • A nested item, set in and marked with a quieter bullet.
    • A second nested item.
  • The last item.
  1. The first step.
  2. The second step, which is long enough to wrap: its second line starts under its first, to the right of the number, at every width.
  3. A step with numbered parts:
    1. The first part.
    2. The second part.
  4. The fourth step.

A list that continues an earlier count keeps its numbers:

  1. The fifth step.
  2. The sixth step.

The archive also has list items that stand outside any list. They are set as the items of a list:

  • A loose item, in bold;
  • A second loose item;
  • A third loose item.

    • The paragraph after them returns to the body rhythm.
